## Executive Summary

The platform has three advantages in market entry:

1. **Government tailwind** — $6B commitment to apprenticeship modernization; explicit mandate for digital logbooks and portable credentials
2. **Union distribution** — direct access to 2,000+ apprentices per local through union partnerships
3. **Credibility through expertise** — built by tradespeople, for tradespeople; not a white-collar platform adapted to trades

The GTM strategy focuses on **institutional buyers first** (unions and apprenticeship boards) while building individual adoption in parallel through a compelling SME knowledge community. This is the inverse of typical SaaS strategy (consumer first, then enterprise) — it's the right order for this market.

**Revenue in Year 1:** $380,000–$580,000 (union subscriptions + government grants)  
**Geographic focus:** Alberta Phase 1 (electricians); Canada Phase 2 (multi-trade expansion)

**Objective:** Secure letter of support from one union local or apprenticeship board; validate product-market fit with 20+ users.

**Why:** A single union letter of support unlocks grant eligibility, distribution, and institutional credibility.

**Stakeholders to identify:**
- Local apprenticeship coordinators (practical decision-makers)
- Union business agents (political/organizational buy-in)
- Provincial apprenticeship board representatives (government relationships)

**How to find them:**

1. **Alberta Electrical League** (construction trades)
   - Contact: Find local IBEW chapters (International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers)
   - Main IBEW locals in Alberta: Local 424 (Edmonton), Local 529 (Calgary), Local 540 (Red Deer)
   - Approach: Start with apprenticeship coordinators, not union leadership

2. **Red Seal Council for Trades & Apprenticeship** (federal level)
   - Contact: ISED Canada (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada)
   - Relevant: These are the people writing the digital logbook mandate
   - Approach: Once you have a union pilot, bring them to Red Seal discussions

3. **Technical colleges in Alberta**
   - SAIT (Southern Alberta Institute of Technology) — trades training
   - NAIT (Northern Alberta Institute of Technology)
   - Contact: Apprenticeship program directors, not admissions

### **Week 2–3: Craft Institutional Pitch**

**What you're pitching:** A pilot program, not a product sale.

**Pitch structure (15 minutes):**

```

"Your apprentices are losing their logbooks. The government is mandating digital

- Apprentices drop out: 40% attrition in trades apprenticeships nationally
- No portable credential record: paper logbooks get lost or left behind
- Mentorship is informal: no structured way to track mentors or verify outcomes
- Government mandate looming: digital logbooks are coming — prepare now

[4 min] The Solution (show, don't tell)
- Demo the onboarding (2 min)
- Show the knowledge community (1 min)
- Show the credential wallet (1 min)

[3 min] The Opportunity for Them
"Partner with us on a pilot. We handle technology. You handle recruitment.

and structured mentorship tracking. Then apply for government funding to scale it."

[2 min] Next Steps
"Let's run a 3-month pilot with 50 apprentices. No cost to you. We provide 
implementation support. You provide access to apprentices and feedback."

Close: "When can we meet to discuss the pilot structure?"
```

### **Week 5–8: Discovery Interviews with 20 Tradespeople**

**Objective:** Validate core UX assumptions before building production-grade features.

**Interview cohort:**

- 5 journeyperson electricians (mix of ages 35–55)
- 5 first-year apprentices (ages 18–25)
- 3 union apprenticeship coordinators
- 3 general contractors (small and mid-size)
- 2 technical college electrician instructors
- 2 Red Seal examiners or apprenticeship board staff

**Interview structure (30 minutes, in-person or video):**

```
[5 min] Intro & framing
"We're building a platform to help apprentices track their learning 
and find mentors. I want to understand how apprenticeships actually work 
and where the pain points are."

[10 min] Current state
- How do apprentices currently track their learning?
- What happens if a logbook is lost?
- How is mentorship typically structured?
- What information would be valuable to have portable?

[10 min] Product reaction
Show the onboarding flow and knowledge community feed.
- Does this make sense?
- Would you use this?
- What's missing?

[5 min] Close
"Thank you. Can I share the platform with you as it develops? 
We'll have something testable in a few weeks."

Collect email, permission to follow up.
```

**Interview analysis template:**

```
Interviewee: [Name, role, company]
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
Key quotes: [3–4 direct quotes that matter]
Pain points identified: [What problems did they mention?]
Product reactions: [Excited, skeptical, indifferent?]
Suggested changes: [Specific feature requests or design feedback]
Likelihood to adopt: [High, medium, low — based on their response]
Next steps: [Invite to pilot, stay in touch, etc.]
```

**Timeline:** Conduct 20 interviews over 4 weeks. Schedule 2–3 per week.

**Recruiting interviews:**

- Post on Reddit r/Electricians or r/Trades asking for interview participants (offer $20 gift card)
- Attend a union meeting or apprenticeship orientation and recruit in person

### **Government Grant Application (Month 6–8)**

**Target programs:**

1. **Union Training and Innovation Program (UTIP)**
   - Up to $75M available for union-led training initiatives
   - Co-applicant: Your union partner
   - Amount: Request $50K–$100K for platform rollout + apprentice incentives
   - Application window: Typically Q2/Q3 each year
   - Status: Check GCOS portal (grants.ca) for current deadlines
   - Contact: Service Canada — Union Training and Innovation Program

2. **Canadian Apprenticeship Strategy — ITE (Investments in Training Equipment)**
   - Up to $5M per project for 36 months
   - Focus: Equipment and technology for apprenticeship training
   - Timing: Applications open Q2/Q3
   - Eligibility: Unions, technical colleges, apprenticeship boards
   - This year's (2026) opportunity: Likely opening soon

**Grant application timeline:**

```
Month 6: Identify program deadlines
Month 7: Draft application with union partner
Month 8: Submit 30 days before deadline
Month 10: Decision (typically 60–90 day turnaround)
```

**Grant application positioning:**

"[Platform] is a digital logbook and credential system that meets the federal government's mandate for portable apprenticeship credentials. With this funding, we will:

- Onboard [X] apprentices across [Y] locals
- Create portable Red Seal credentials eliminating paper logbooks
- Build structured mentorship tracking
- Measure impact on apprentice retention and time to certification

By [end date], we will have demonstrated that digital apprenticeship infrastructure works at scale in Canada, positioning the trades industry for the Red Seal digital transformation that is already mandated."

**Grant-funded outcomes you'll report:**

- Number of apprentices onboarded
- Apprentices who achieved certification
- Time saved on administrative tasks (hours)
- Credentials issued (portable badges)
- Mentorship relationships established
- Retention improvement vs. baseline

## Risk Mitigation

### **Risk 1: Union doesn't respond or says no**

**Mitigation:**
- Widen outreach (don't rely on first local)
- If "no" — ask why; address objection; stay in touch (12-month follow-up)
- Parallel path: Approach technical college or apprenticeship board (same problem, different buyer)

### **Risk 2: Grant applications rejected**

**Mitigation:**
- Start grant applications in Month 6, not Month 12 (more time to adapt if rejected)
- Get union partner to help with application (they have credibility with government)
- Research what funded in past years; model your ask on those
- If rejected: "We'll improve and reapply next cycle" — grants take persistence

### **Risk 3: User adoption is slow (< 100 active after 6 months)**

**Mitigation:**
- Survey pilot users: "What's getting in the way of adoption?"
- Simplify onboarding (2 minutes, not 15)
- Create inbound motivation: Seed the platform with great questions, have mentors answer them
- Partner with instructors: Make the platform part of the apprenticeship curriculum

### **Risk 4: Competitors emerge (LinkedIn, Workday, etc.)**

**Mitigation:**
- Your moat is trades-specificity + union relationships, not technology
- Move fast to government integration (that's hard to replicate)
- Build community defensively: Strong mentorship culture = hard to poach users
