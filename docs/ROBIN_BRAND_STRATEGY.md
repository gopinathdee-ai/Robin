# Robin — Brand Strategy Document

**Trades Credential & Mentorship Platform**  
**Version 1.0 | May 2026 | Shoestring Budget Edition**

---

## **The Problem We Solve**

Two opposite problems with the same solution:

### **Steven's Problem (Age 20, Apprentice Electrician)**
Steven knows he can do the work. He's been doing residential electrical for 2 years — new builds, renovations, complex systems. But his current employer won't acknowledge his competence because it would mean paying him more or losing him to another company.

**He feels: Stuck.** Like there's no way forward without leaving everything behind.

**What he needs:** Proof. Multiple ways to prove what he can do — certifications, recommendations, a portfolio of projects, references from people who actually know his work — so he can show other employers "this is what I'm capable of" and get out.

**What he wants:** To be discoverable. To find better job opportunities based on verified competence, not just luck and connections.

---

### **The Mentor's Problem (Journeyperson or Contractor, 15+ years)**
They want to mentor or hire good people. But they're drowning in resumes. Everyone claims they can do the work. No way to actually know who's competent without hiring them first.

**They need:** Confidence. A way to see who's actually skilled, who's been mentored well, who has a track record of real competence.

**What they want:** To find mentees or employees worth betting on. To build a team they can trust.

---

### **Robin's Solution**
**Your skills are visible. Your record is verifiable. You're not trapped by what your current employer will admit.**

For Steven: Proof of competence → freedom to move forward  
For the Mentor: Visible competence → confidence to hire and mentor

## **Brand Positioning**

**Positioning Statement:**
> For Canadian tradespeople and the people who hire them, Robin is the platform that makes skills visible and verifiable—unlike paper logbooks and LinkedIn which hide competence behind outdated records and noise.

**Human Version:**
> Steven knows he can do the work. His company just won't say it. Robin lets him prove it—to other employers, to mentors, to himself. When contractors need to hire someone they can actually trust, Robin shows them who's really skilled.

## **Brand Personality**

Robin feels like a **workshop where people show and respect their work**.

### **Core Brand Attributes:**

**Warm and Worn**
- Like tools that have been used and cared for
- Not shiny and new, not polished and sterile
- Evidence of real work, real use
- Trustworthy because it's been tested

**Collaborative & Honest**
- People helping each other get better
- No ego, no BS — just craft
- Mistakes and fixes are visible side by side
- You can see what's actually working

**Straightforward**
- No corporate jargon, no tech speak
- Say what you mean
- Cut through the noise
- Practical over aspirational

**Respectful**
- Respect for the work itself
- Respect for experience earned through doing
- Respect for competence as the only currency
- No shortcuts, no fakes

## **Voice & Tone**

When Robin talks to users:

✅ **Simple, clear communication** — No flowery language or corporate-speak. Electricians and contractors should understand immediately.

✅ **Show, don't tell** — Let the work speak. Show portfolios, endorsements, real credentials. Don't make claims.

✅ **Direct and practical** — Explain the "why" when needed, but keep it brief. Users are busy working.

**Examples:**

❌ Bad: "Unlock your potential and join a community of forward-thinking trades professionals."  
✅ Good: "Show what you can do. Get hired for it."

❌ Bad: "Seamlessly integrate your credential portfolio across platforms."  
✅ Good: "Your record travels with you."

❌ Bad: "Leverage your expertise to build mentorship relationships."  
✅ Good: "Help the next person. Get recognized for it."

## **Key Messages**

When someone thinks of Robin, they should remember:

### **1. Your record belongs to you**
Your skills record isn't locked in with one employer. It follows you across jobs, provinces, your whole career.

### **2. Competence is visible**
You can show what you can do. No more hiding good work or pretending you're better than you are. Work speaks.

### **3. Trust is verifiable**
When you're hiring or mentoring, you see actual competence. No more guessing. You can see recommendations from people who've worked with someone.

### **4. Built for trades, by people who understand trades**
We get it. We're not here to "disrupt" or lecture. We built this because we know how the work actually happens.

## **Visual Direction Keywords**

These guide every design decision that comes next:

- **Solid** — not playful or trendy
- **Warm** — orange that feels like safety vests and visibility, not corporate blue
- **Real** — actual hands, actual tools, job site reality (not stock photos)
- **Clear** — readable on a phone in bright sunlight on a job site
- **Accessible** — works for a 20-year-old and a 55-year-old equally
- **Honest** — evidence of use, not polished perfection

## **What We Are NOT**

- Not Silicon Valley startup energy
- Not "disruption" rhetoric
- Not social media gamification
- Not corporate or institutional
- Not condescending (never talk down to trades workers)
- Not aspirational in the "dream bigger" sense — aspirational in the "do real work and get recognized for it" sense

## **Competitive Differentiation**

| Platform | Their Strength | Their Weakness | Robin's Advantage |
|----------|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Professional network | Designed for white-collar professionals; credentials are self-declared; noisy | Purpose-built for trades; earned credentials from peers; trades hierarchy language |
| Paper logbooks | Portable by nature | Lost, damaged, forgotten; no verification | Digital + portable + verifiable |
| Employer systems | Company-specific data | Locked in with employer; disappears when you leave | Individually owned; follows you everywhere |
| Generic "community" platforms | Broad reach | No credibility verification; noise; not trades-specific | Peer endorsements from verified equals; domain-specific knowledge |

## **What This Means for Design & Experience**

(These principles guide every screen, button, and decision)

### **Show Real Work**
- Actual job site photos, not stock images
- Real people of diverse ages
- Evidence of projects completed
- Before/after, process shots

### **Make Competence Visible**
- Endorsements from verified peers are prominent
- Project portfolios are easy to build and share
- Credentials are immediately visible
- No hiding behind vague titles

### **Keep It Practical**
- Every feature solves a real problem (hiring, proving skills, mentoring)
- No features that exist just to be "engaging"
- Works on a job site (readable in sunlight, usable with dirty hands)
- Fast — people are busy

### **Be Honest About Limitations**
- Show what you DON'T know as clearly as what you DO
- Endorsements can't be faked
- Reputation builds over time (no shortcuts)
- Some things take work to verify

### **Respect Experience**
- A 55-year-old master electrician's opinion matters more than a 20-year-old apprentice's
- That's not elitism — that's how trades actually work
- Design for that reality

## **This Is Your North Star**

Every time you (or a designer, or developers) make a decision about Robin, test it against this:

**Does this serve Steven's need to prove himself and escape being trapped?**

**Does this help the Mentor confidently find people worth investing in?**

**Does it feel like a workshop where work is shown and respected?**

If yes → do it.  
If no → ask why you're doing it.
