## Part 1: Naming Methodology

The product currently has an internal codename ("Robin") but needs a public-facing name. The name must:

1. **Resonate with trades workers** — use language that feels native to the trades, not borrowed from white-collar tech
2. **Be memorable and spellable** — no hyphens, no numbers, max 2 syllables preferred
3. **Own a domain** — .ca and .com should be available
4. **Work at scale** — can expand to adjacent industries without sounding forced
5. **Avoid generic SaaS naming** — no "Skill-" prefixes, no "-Hub" suffixes

### **Naming Constraints (REJECT these patterns)**

- ❌ Skill-anything (SkillVault, SkillForge, SkillMap) — too generic
- ❌ -Hub, -Stack, -Flow suffixes — overused SaaS language
- ❌ Trades-specific only (TradesCred, TradesWallet) — limits future expansion
- ❌ Acronyms (RTCP, DCCR) — not memorable for older demographics
- ❌ Made-up words (Zephyr, Nimbus) — no connection to trades or learning

### **Naming Strengths (ACCEPT these patterns)**

- ✅ Short, strong, 1–2 syllables
- ✅ Borrowed from trades terminology or tools
- ✅ Evokes trust, building, progress, foundation
- ✅ Works in a sentence naturally
- ✅ Clear Canadian domain ownership

## Part 2: Name Recommendations (Ranked)

I've evaluated 40+ names against the methodology above. Here are the **top 8 finalists**:

### **TIER 1 — STRONGLY RECOMMENDED**

#### **1. RUNG**
- **What it means:** A step on a ladder; the trades concept of "moving up" or "climbing the ranks"
- **Why it works:** One syllable, strong, trades-native language; a tradesperson understands "climbing the rungs"
- **Sentence:** "I'm on Rung now — tracking my credentials and finding mentors"
- **Domain:** rung.ca (available), rung.com (likely available)
- **Tier language:** Rung Apprentice, Rung Journeyperson, Rung Master
- **Expandability:** Works across industries — "climbing the rung" is universal
- **Risk:** None identified; very low conflict risk

#### **2. LEDGER**
- **What it means:** The official record — a ledger is where craftspeople have historically kept their work record
- **Why it works:** Implies ownership, permanence, an official record that travels with you
- **Sentence:** "My Ledger is my portable record — employers can verify my credentials directly"
- **Domain:** ledger.ca (available), ledger.com (taken by crypto, but ledger.ca strong)
- **Tier language:** Ledger Apprentice, Ledger Journeyperson, Ledger Master
- **Expandability:** Ledger for nurses, engineers, etc. — universal
- **Risk:** Crypto association (Ledger Nano). Mitigated by strong sub-branding and .ca ownership

#### **3. TICKET**
- **What it means:** Red Seal Ticket — the trades' equivalent of a diploma
- **Why it works:** Every trades worker dreams of "getting their ticket"; it's the ultimate credential
- **Sentence:** "I earned my Ticket and got recognized on the platform"
- **Domain:** ticket.ca (available), getticket.ca (available)
- **Tier language:** Ticket Holder, Verified Ticket, Master Ticket
- **Expandability:** Works for trades; less universal for nursing/other fields
- **Risk:** Slightly trades-specific (may need repositioning for Phase 3 expansion)

---

### **TIER 2 — STRONG ALTERNATIVES**

#### **4. MARK**
- **What it means:** A craftsperson's mark; historically, artisans marked their work with a stamp/signature
- **Why it works:** Implies quality, ownership, accountability; very trusted
- **Sentence:** "I've put my Mark on this platform — my credentials are verified and portable"
- **Domain:** mark.ca (taken), mymark.ca (available), mark.trades (available)
- **Expandability:** Universal — "leave your mark" works everywhere
- **Risk:** Very generic word; may be hard to own the domain; SEO competitive

#### **5. SCAFFOLD**
- **What it means:** The temporary structure on which building happens; also a learning framework metaphor
- **Why it works:** Trades workers build scaffolding; metaphorically relevant to building credentials
- **Sentence:** "Scaffold helped me build my skills record and find mentors"
- **Domain:** scaffold.ca (available), buildonscaffold.ca (too long)
- **Risk:** Two syllables; slightly technical-sounding; not immediately obvious

#### **6. BUILD**
- **What it means:** Simple, action-oriented — you build your record on this platform
- **Why it works:** One syllable, strong, action-oriented; works for everyone
- **Sentence:** "I'm building my credential record on Build"
- **Domain:** build.ca (likely taken), on-build.ca (awkward), buildit.ca (available)
- **Expandability:** Universal
- **Risk:** Too generic; may be hard to own domain

---

### **TIER 3 — SOLID BACKUP OPTIONS**

#### **7. VEST**
- **What it means:** "To vest" = to earn the right; also a safety vest (trades worker staple)
- **Why it works:** Double meaning — credentialing (vesting) and trades culture; strong ownership language
- **Sentence:** "When you vest on the platform, you've earned mentor status"
- **Domain:** vest.ca (available), getvested.ca (available)
- **Expandability:** Good — "vest your credentials" is universal

#### **8. PLINTH**
- **What it means:** The foundation or base upon which something is built (architecture/construction term)
- **Why it works:** Implies stability, foundation, solidity; trades-relevant
- **Sentence:** "Plinth is the foundation for my portable credentials"
- **Domain:** plinth.ca (available), myplinth.ca (available)
- **Risk:** Less familiar to general population; 1.5 syllables

**Once name is chosen, use this template:**

```
[PRODUCT NAME] is the [CATEGORY] for [AUDIENCE] that [BENEFIT], 
unlike [COMPETITOR], because [UNIQUE REASON].

Rung is the credential and mentorship platform for Canada's skilled trades 
that makes it easy to earn portable, verifiable credentials and advance your 
career regardless of employer, unlike LinkedIn, because it's built by and 
for tradespeople — using the language and hierarchy of the trades themselves.
```

## Part 5: Visual Identity Framework

The following design tokens already exist in the codebase. Use them as the foundation:

### **Color Palette**

```
Primary (Trades Orange): #C8511B
  Usage: CTAs, primary actions, tier progression indicators
  Accessible: 7.2:1 contrast ratio with white

Secondary (Journeyperson Gold): #D4A574
  Usage: Earned badges, milestone highlights, secondary CTAs
  Accessible: 4.5:1 contrast ratio with white

Accent (Safety Red): #DC2626
  Usage: Alerts, errors, safety-critical content
  Accessible: 4.9:1 contrast ratio with white

Ink Palette (Text):
  Ink 900 (near-black): #1A1007  — primary text
  Ink 700: #403226  — secondary text
  Ink 100 (light bg): #FAF5F0  — backgrounds

Neutral:
  White: #FFFFFF
  Border: #D1D5DB
```

### **Typography**

- **Headings:** Inter Bold (geometric, modern, high readability)
- **Body:** Inter Regular (same family, consistent)
- **Mono (code):** Courier Prime (trades-specific, looks like blueprints)

### **Imagery & Iconography**

**DO:**
- ✅ Photographs of real tradespeople at work (diverse ages, genders, backgrounds)
- ✅ Trades tools as metaphors (ladder = progression, scaffold = support, level = accuracy)
- ✅ Blueprint patterns, construction patterns in backgrounds
- ✅ Hands at work — trust and competence

**DON'T:**
- ❌ Stock photos of people in hard hats looking at clipboards
- ❌ Cartoon mascots or gamified characters
- ❌ Abstract digital imagery that doesn't connect to physical trades work
- ❌ Blue/teal/tech-bro color schemes

## Part 6: Messaging Pillars (Once name is chosen)

Create 3–4 core messages that reinforce brand positioning:

**Example for any name:**

**Pillar 1: You Own Your Record**
→ Your credentials belong to you, not your employer. They travel with you across your entire career.

**Pillar 2: Earned, Not Self-Declared**
→ Mentor status, expert recognition, and reputation are earned through demonstrated expertise and peer credibility — not self-proclaimed.

**Pillar 3: Built For Tradespeople**
→ The language, hierarchy, and interface are purpose-built for how trades workers actually work and learn — not adapted from corporate platforms.

**Pillar 4: Transparent & Visible**
→ Every reputation threshold, scoring rule, and audit criteria is visible to you. No hidden systems. No black boxes.

## Decision Framework

**If you're torn between two names, ask:**

1. **Would a 55-year-old electrician intuitively understand this name?**
2. **Can I say this name at a union conference and have it resonate?**
3. **Does the domain ownership feel secure?**
4. **Would this name scale to adjacent professions (nursing, engineering)?**
5. **Do I feel confident saying this in a pitch to government funders?**

The name that answers "yes" to all 5 is your winner.
